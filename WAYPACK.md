# WayPack

GPS creature-collecting game engine for the phone: real map, live GPS, the world as a fixed grid of routes; all creature content comes from loadable packs. Current build: `index.html` in the repo (development name `scouter-world-v90.html`; needs `forge-engine.js` beside it for music) (single file, no build step, Leaflet + OpenStreetMap tiles). Harness: `waypack.tests.mjs`, 337 checks against the shipped file, including the real Pokémon pack.

Name chosen 2026-09-04: **WayPack** (waypoints + packs). Rejected: Wayfarer (Niantic's mapping platform, a live AR exploration game, and several store apps share it), Wanderlands (a Play Store app, a dormant studio, a Roblox game). WayPack not yet searched for collisions; do that before any store listing.

## Pillars

1. The world is where you are. Routes, domains, regions and gyms are fixed to the real map; you walk to things.
2. The app owns no creature IP. Packs do. The engine ships clean; packs load from files or URLs.
3. Every rule is proven by the harness before it ships, and balance claims are measured, not asserted.

## World model (settled)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Route | One grid square, 500 m or 250 m (toggle). Exact north–south; east–west exact at 43.45°N (Kitchener), drifts elsewhere. One habitat family, one rare family, 1–3 trainers per day. Numbered within its region. |
| Domain | A block of routes sharing a gym type. Holds one gym on a random route of the domain. |
| Region | 3×3 domains. Named (generated, franchise names blocklisted). 8 gyms + a League. Own history, lore, legends. |

Layouts (menu, per device): **Domains** = domains are 2×2 routes, League domain at the centre. **Wide** = domains are 3×3 routes, League domain placed at random. **Dense** = a region is 3×3 routes, every route a gym except one random League route. All layouts ask for 8 badges.

Gym types: distinct within a region, and never shared with an edge-adjacent region (the type pool is split into two halves by checkerboard parity; packs with fewer than 16 types fall back to within-region distinctness). Leader names: distinct within a region and per type across a 5×5 block of regions; pack pools under 20 names are topped up with the engine's per-type name pools.

## Systems (settled)

**Battles.** Pack decides the mode. `light` (the official GMS pack): each creature has at most three attacks plus Guard, from pack data: Strike in its first type (STAB 1.2), Strike in its second type if dual-typed (a taught TM replaces this slot; a mono-typed creature gains it), and a signature by power tier (power ≤0 Quick Jab 0.7× always first; 1–2 Heavy Blow 1.6× at 70%; ≥3 or legendary Unleash 2.2× once per battle). The AI picks by expected damage. Measured: where the two Strikes differ against the foe, picking the right one wins 27% vs 2%; a Water TM takes Pikachu vs Geodude from 0% to 96%. Wind-ups as before: the wild sometimes winds up (2.5× next hit); guarding a wind-up cuts damage to 25% and arms a 2× counter; 92% hit, 10% crit. Measured: reading wind-ups 93% vs mashing 37% at equal level; reads win about a third of −3 fights. `full` (PokéAPI-built packs): standard level-scaled stats, physical/special, STAB, chart, PP, speed order, AI by expected damage.

**Gyms.** Each region has its own scale: leaders start at Lv 9–14 and gain 4–6 per badge you hold (hashed per region); the Champion is max(50, base + step × 8 + 4) + 2 × badges, so 50–66 at zero badges. The scale is not shown to the player; they discover it at the gym door, where the leader's current level appears in the header and on the challenge button, and in their own records of beaten leaders. Team = 2 + badges/2, cap 5 (2,2,3,3,4,4,5,5). Every generated team obeys the pack's evolution levels: a member is legal only at or above its range floor, type is applied after legality, and a family with no legal on-type member at that level is skipped for another (no Lv 15 Pangoro). One ace, +3 levels, on a fixed evolution line whose base member carries the gym's type, evolving as badges grow. After the first win only, the player may choose the ace's line from the team they beat and give it a nickname, which the leader's ace then carries into every rematch. Badge once; rematches unlimited, and every beaten team is kept in the gym's record with the leader's level at the time. A menu setting, Battle at the leader's level (Off, Equal, Leader −1, −3, −5), caps the player's own creatures for gym, League, master, defence, tournament and road battles to the leader's level plus the offset: stats and HP scale down for the fight (HP kept as a fraction), XP still goes to the real level, and the cap lifts when the battle ends or the app reloads. Leaders themselves always follow the region's scale. Leaders have intro/win/loss/rematch lines.

**League.** Elite Four (distinct types, six each, champion −4/−3/−3/−2 by placement) then the Champion (six, level 45 + 3 × badges). Becoming Champion unlocks the region's legends.

**Title defence.** Every 4-hour slot, 50% chance a contender waits at the League while you hold the title: a region leader (35%), a current Elite (25%), a strong trainer above every leader's level (25%), or the former champion with the same team +3 levels (15%). Lose and they become Champion with the exact team they used; a new Elite Four assembles; each failed reclaim adds 2 levels to the usurper. Held gyms also draw challengers (50% of days) for the badge; defending pays coins, losing costs nothing but the day.

**Legends.** Up to 3 per region per month from the rare families on its routes; re-rolled on the 1st, caught ones out for the month. One is on a route each day (marker + header line); one attempt per region per day; fixed fight at max(pack minimum level, team level). After a flee/run/wipe the menu setting decides: **Stay** (same region tomorrow) or **Roam** (edge-adjacent region, keeps moving until caught). Lore names a legend's route only when it is there today. Random scans no longer produce legendaries.

**Environment.** The app detects iOS, which browser it is in (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Opera, Safari), whether it is running from the Home Screen, and whether it is the native shell. On iOS every browser is WebKit but each keeps its own location permission, and a Home Screen copy asks for its own; when no fix arrives the advice names the actual browser and offers the Home Screen route, which is what fixed it on Casim's phone. iPhone players in a tab see the Home Screen suggestion once, and it stays available in the menu. Cards never interrupt each other or a battle: they queue until the screen is free.

**GPS honesty.** `watchPosition` carries a 20 s timeout and a 30 s watchdog, so a phone that never delivers a fix is reported rather than silently leaving the player on the default start coordinates. The pill states the truth (GPS off, finding you, the reason it failed, ±N m, or rough) and retries when tapped; the player dot is faded until a real fix lands. Fixes worse than 60 m are held back for 25 s in case a better one arrives, then used for the map only, with walking credit suspended and a note on turning on Precise Location; a sharp fix restores it. This replaces the old rule that discarded every fix over 60 m, which froze the dot on phones with imprecise location.

**Walking.** 1 coin per 25 m. +1 Pokéball I per 300 m. Eggs found about every 1.6 km (cap 3), hatch after the pack's cycles × 150 m, weighted by the pack's `eggFrequency`. Walking credit only below 7 m/s; position follows any fix under 60 m/s; fixes worse than 60 m ignored.

**Shop.** Staples (Pokéball I/II, Tonic, Revive) + 4 rotating slots from Pokéball III, Full Tonic and the pack's evolution items; prices ±25% daily, one half-price item. Heal-all costs 30 coins.

**Items and forms.** Wild drops per pack probabilities; evolution items usable from the team panel. Creatures carry gender, gene (regional variant, 15% where the pack has one), a held item and walked distance; the pack's forms resolve from those plus the clock (Alolan variants, gender forms, Mega forms by held stone, hour/season forms), changing name, types, power, sprite, stats and moves. Conditional evolutions: level, gene, gender, hour, season, happiness (5 km walked together), distance, TM, coins; item evolutions as Use buttons. Shop sells evolution items, form items and TMs.

**Trainers.** 1–3 per route per day from the pack's trainer classes with their conditions; overworld icons on the map, battle sprite as portrait; teams from the route family at the player's level; 1.5× XP and 2–4 catch items.

**End-to-end sweep.** `waypack-sweep.cjs` drives the whole app in a headless browser (pack load, starter, wild and trainer and gym battles, catching, every Team/Region/Online/menu tab, dex, photo, shop, directions with all modes and voice, grid and region views, all guide topics, slot switching, export and import, a partial reset, connection check, reload) and fails on any JavaScript or console error. First run found two real regressions, both fixed and now covered by checks: a missing shared purse read as zero coins, so every new playthrough started broke with the shop greyed out; and closing the team photo left the player on the map instead of back in the Team panel. Battle steps in the sweep must re-query buttons each turn, since the battle card rebuilds.

**Menu.** Five tabs with the last one remembered: Playing (position, screen awake, low power, level cap, legends, the two voices, effects, music, alerts), World (map style, habitat squares, layout with its six playthroughs, route size, migration, wanderers), Places (pins, offline map, Home Screen, connection), Data (pack, badge pool, backup, reset) and Help (how to play, display). Every control sits in exactly one tab and each tab fits a phone screen without scrolling, checked by walking all thirty controls.

**Screen and background.** A web page receives no location once the screen locks or the browser is backgrounded; no setting inside the page changes that, and only a native shell can. The Screen Wake Lock API is the one lever the platform gives, so **Keep the screen awake** holds the screen on while playing (re-acquired when the tab returns to view) and says plainly that a locked screen stops tracking; where the API is missing it points at Auto-Lock instead. Driving still earns nothing, since walking credit stops above a brisk run.

**Offline and power.** Every tile drawn is kept in IndexedDB (cap 4,000) and served when the network is gone, proven by a reload with the tile server unreachable: all visible tiles drew from the cache with zero requests. **Low power** takes a fix about every 20 s instead of continuously, stops fetching tiles, pauses music and online checks. **Connection** in the menu names each outside service (tiles, landmarks, directions, address search, online play) as answering, not reachable or not used, with the session's minutes, kilometres and map data. Every outside call reports through one status layer, and a service going down says once what still works instead of failing quietly. **What changed** appears once after a version bump, with a link into the guide.

**Forms survive capture (v88).** Before v84 an encounter carried no form: the wild creature's variant came from `creatureTraits(enc.seed)` and the caught one from `creatureTraits(save.nextUid)`, two different rolls, so a Galarian Darumaka usually arrived home as an ordinary Darumaka (Casim hit exactly this). Since v84 the encounter carries `gene`/`adapt` and the catch copies it; verified over 25 real encounters, 25 kept both name and typing. Creatures caught on older builds cannot be recovered, since nothing recorded what they were.

**Duels (v106).** Asynchronous and symmetric: both players fight the other's saved team, settled on creatures standing then turns taken, draws returning the stakes. A duel battle draws from `duelRand(code, turn)` rather than `Math.random`, so it runs identically on both phones; a report therefore carries the player's own choices, the opponent's game replays them with `duelReplay` against the snapshot it holds, and `wp_duel_verify` settles only when each side has confirmed the other. A report that does not replay is disputed: no coins, no record. Reports cannot be revised once given, stakes are capped at what the player holds and at 500, and a duel carries the pack fingerprint like a trade. Duels have their own standing (`wp_duel_standing`) kept apart from badges and shinies. Tested with two browsers against a stand-in server: a mirror match drew, a Lv 40 team beat a Lv 12 team 3 standing to 0, and the loser paid 50 coins while the winner was credited on their next visit. That last part was a real bug found in testing: the sweep only handled duels in `checking`, so a duel settled by the other side while you were away paid nobody.

**Seasons and the bulletin (v104).** `recordSeasonTitle` stamps a League win against `seasonOf(now)` in `save.seasons[regionKey@season]`, counting repeat wins in the same season without a second entry, so a region can be taken again as seasons turn; Progress lists them newest first. `seasonLabel` names them (December belongs to the winter that follows). `leagueBulletin` appears only while standing on a League and reads the region's own state: badges and title, the week's hunt family and watched legend, the team at work with its aim, the player's season across regions, and neighbouring regions visited.

**Pack authoring kit (v104-era, tooling).** `pack-starter/` is a loadable eight-family pack of original creatures; `make-placeholder-sprites.mjs` writes one distinct PNG per species with no dependencies so a pack can be walked before any art exists; `pack-check.mjs` runs the engine's own validator plus the checks it cannot make (missing or unused art, dangling evolutions, families with no first stage, undeclared families, duplicate and over-long names) and exits non-zero; `PACK-AUTHORING.md` is the walkthrough. `pack-build.mjs` had been pinned to `scouter-world-v39.html` for sixty versions and now finds whichever build sits beside it.

**Trade integrity (v103).** Every trade carries `packFingerprint()`, a hash over every species id, name, typing, stats, evolutions and forms, plus habitat and gym-type counts. `wp_trade_take` refuses a mismatch, so an altered pack can neither offer nor take. Tested against real tampering: custom stats, custom typing, a rename and an added species are each caught, and reverting restores the original fingerprint. Incoming creatures are rebuilt from the receiver's pack, and an unknown species now stops the trade before the taker gives anything up. (While building this I overwrote the live trade functions with guessed column names and had to restore them from the real schema.)

**Per-feature grants (v101).** Tiers were dropped for individual entitlements: `wp_supporters.items` is a text array, `wp_grant_items(secret, handle, friend_code, items, mode)` adds, removes or sets them, and the client only reads `wp_my_items`. Action-style features check `donorHas` (the grant) rather than `donorOn` (the grant plus a switch), since an action has no switch to find. The starter screen waits for the first entitlement check, capped at 2.5 s, so a new supporter is not shown the three regional starters instead of the whole pack.

**Week card and palettes (v102, free).** `weekCard()` draws the last seven days at 1080x1080 with the distance walked as the headline, shared as a PNG through `navigator.share` where files are allowed, falling back to a download and then to text. An adapted form gets five shades within its own type's hue range, chosen per creature and saved on it.

**Supporter items, part four (v100).** Fifteen items now. Route notes (`myNote`, deliberately not `routeNote`, which is the spoken route line) pin 120 characters to a route and show them on the HUD only while standing there. The fourth egg raises the carrying limit by one (verified: at three eggs a non-supporter finds none, a supporter finds a fourth, and at four even a supporter finds none). `makeRestStop` converts one landmark a week into a permanent rest stop that heals like any other (verified: the first succeeds, the second in the same week is refused). The chosen starter accepts any species in the pack that nothing evolves into, so it must be a first stage.

**Route memory and named boxes (v99, free).** `noteScan` keeps the last twenty scan spots with the species seen, merging repeat scans at the same route within a minute rather than filling the list, de-duplicating species, and dropping the family name where it just repeats the species. Each row gives distance, bearing and Directions back. The box splits into player-named groups (`m.box`, 18 characters) with tabs for each group and Unfiled, filtered by `state.boxFilter`. Casim asked for both to be free rather than supporter items.

**Two worlds at hand, offline regions (v98).** Eleven supporter items now. `worldab` pins up to two seeds and `flipWorld` switches with `applyWorldSeed(seed, true)`, skipping the confirmation since both sides' records are kept anyway. `offline` downloads a region: `landmarksFor` first (one Overpass request, the part the game needs most), then tiles across `regionBounds` at zooms 14–17 into the existing tile cache, 12 ms apart because the OSM tile server is a volunteer service, skipping tiles already held, counting failures rather than throwing, and stoppable mid-run keeping what came down. A Domains region is 318 tiles at those zooms, about 5.6 MB, and the button states the count and size before starting. Both verified in the browser (29 tiles fetched and readable back from cache, a stopped run keeping its progress, a flip landing back on the starting world) and both refuse at tier 0.

**Switching and experience (v97).** Experience was already paid per foe to whoever was out, not pooled at the end (traced: A took 135 for the first Rattata, fell, B came in and took 147 for the second). What was missing was any way to choose who fought: there was no voluntary switch, so one creature usually banked a whole trainer's team. `switchTo` brings another creature out and spends the turn (`act({switched:true})` with a `skip` action the light engine understands, so the opponent acts and the player does not), verified by B taking 5 damage on arrival. Switch buttons get their own row, fainted creatures are not offered. `XPSHARE` then offers three modes, defaulting to the old behaviour: 'one' (135/0/0), 'half' (135/68/68) and 'all' (135/135/135), with fainted creatures excluded and a level-up from a share announced in the log.

**Route trainers (v95).** Three faults, all fixed. The class picker took the *first* class whose conditions fitted, so two or three titles dominated; it now weights every fitting class, downweighting a title above the level (1/(1+over·0.6)) and one far below it (1/(1+under·0.12)), with the pack's own `prob` still applied. The pack gates classes by `minlvl`, which left every early trainer a Preschooler; `CLASS_REACH` (level·2.2 + 8) lets a route reach a little above itself, so a Lv 8 route fields 16 titles instead of 2. Measured: 16 titles early, 35 at Lv 30, 36 at Lv 70, with Preschooler commonest early and Veteran at the top. Teams were 1–3 by level alone and are now simply 1–4, an even roll made when the trainer is generated and independent of badges (measured 25% each at 0, 4 and 8 badges; v95 briefly weighted this by badges, which Casim corrected), and 45% of each team is drawn from a random route anywhere in the region rather than the one underfoot (measured 42%), so a trainer can show a family the player has not walked to. Levels still track team level and badges (6 → 32 → 72 across the three surveys) and a route is still identical within a day.

**Supporter items, part two (v94).** Six more, all gated the same way and all confined to the holder's own game. Incubator doubles egg walking credit (600 m becomes 1,200, and 600 without the tier). Photo frames: five, drawn onto the finished picture beneath the caption. Second anchor raises the starting-pin slots from one to two, so two pins can both be travelled to. Second look allows exactly one landmark repeat a day, spent on the first reuse (`againPending`/`againDay`). Gym callout brings the domain's leader to the current route once a day, with `state.calloutNow` standing in for `d.atGym` so the badge and the battle are otherwise identical. Legend watch overrides which legend a region watches, keyed to that region and week so it cannot be hoarded, and still exactly one is watched. All verified in the browser; CORE remains free of every one of them.

**Supporter items (v93).** `DONOR_ITEMS` names each item with the tier it needs; `donorHas` gates on the tier and `donorOn` also requires the switch, so flipping the stored setting without a tier does nothing (verified: forcing `DONOR.charm = true` at tier 0 leaves it off). Shiny Charm sets the odds to 1 in 125 (measured 1 in 127 against 1 in 494 off) and marks every creature it finds with `charm: true`, for life. A charm find is filtered out of the trade and gift picker and `tradeBlockReason` explains why, so a supporter's advantage cannot reach another player's collection: this is what makes gameplay items defensible here rather than cosmetics only. Shiny Lock suppresses the flee roll for shinies alone. The Level Selector narrows the wild level band but is trimmed to the badge ceiling, so it can never raise levels. The trainer card gains a tier-coloured double frame and a mark, and counts charm finds apart from ordinary shinies. The CORE engine contains none of this: with no supporter layer present the base rate stands (measured 1 in 492), which the harness asserts.

**Supporter tiers (v92).** Payment happens outside the game (e-transfer), so the grant is the record of truth. `wp_supporters` holds one row per player, tier 0 to 5. `wp_grant_tier(secret, handle, friend_code, tier, note)` sets it, guarded by an admin secret in `private.wp_admin` that never touches the app; the name is matched case-insensitively and the friend code must match, so a wrong code is refused rather than granting a stranger's trainer. `wp_mint_codes` makes 10-character codes for gifts and `wp_redeem_code` spends one, single-use, case-insensitive, and never lowering a tier already held. The client only ever calls `wp_my_tier` and `wp_redeem_code`: it cannot grant anything. The tier is cached on the device and refreshed on every touch, so a grant made while the player is out walking arrives by itself. Eleven backend checks and five client checks cover it. **No benefit is attached to a tier yet** — that is Casim's decision, and the harness asserts the game reads the tier without acting on it.

**Battle buttons (v91).** Attacks and catch items were one wrapping row, so a creature with fewer moves shifted the balls left and an Ultra Ball could go on a tap meant for an attack (Casim lost one this way). They are now two rows with a divider, items and Run below; measured identical coordinates for the Ultra Ball at two and three attacks. One Ultra Ball is returned once per save via `ballAmends`.

**Quickest or shortest (v90).** OSRM is asked for alternatives; the quickest and the shortest are computed and the player picks, with the panel naming what the other costs ("Shortest: 6.0 km, 15 min"). Free and needs no key.

**Live traffic (v90).** No free routing service publishes live conditions, so a driving time from the FOSSGIS routers is the road's usual speed. A player can paste their own Mapbox token under Places, Live traffic; driving then uses `driving-traffic` with congestion annotations, the duration is real, and the map line is split into runs coloured green through red. Walking and cycling never use it, a traffic failure falls back to the free routers, and the token stays on the device. Verified against a mocked provider: one call, seven congestion runs from low through severe.

**Sprite repainting (v89).** A CSS hue rotation drags existing colours around the wheel and muddies anything already near the target, so adapted sprites are now redrawn pixel by pixel on a canvas: each pixel converts to HSL, its hue is moved to sit around the type's hue while keeping 45% of its offset from the sprite's own dominant hue (so two-tone creatures stay two-tone and shading survives), and the type's saturation, brightness or grey is applied. Results are cached per sprite and tint, baked once asynchronously with the CSS tint standing in meanwhile, and any failure falls back to the original rather than losing the sprite.

**Form palettes (v88).** `TYPE_TINT` gives each of the pack's 18 types a hue range plus saturation, brightness or grey where the type reads as more than a hue: steel dull, dark deep, ice pale, electric bright. An adapted form's rotation is drawn from its own type's range, so it always looks like what it is. Fighting and normal take the full wheel deliberately.

**Adapted forms (v87).** Beyond the pack's official variants, a region can grow its own: `adaptedTypes()` draws typing from the pack's gym types excluding the original's, `adaptedName()` prefixes the region's name, `adaptedHue()` gives a palette rotation applied to the sprite in rows and battle, and the stats take their own redistribution. Deterministic per species, region and world; about 3.9% of encounters, never combined with an official variant. A creature stores only `adapt: "gx,gy"`, so the form is recomputed rather than duplicated.

**Form stats (v87).** `variantStats` now always moves health, because that is the only stat a team row shows and forms otherwise read identically (Casim reported exactly this). The gains and losses are rebalanced so the total is unchanged, verified at 357 against 357.

**The blank-panel bug (v82 to v85, fixed v86).** The v82 weekly notice called `esc()`, a private helper inside `renderMp`, from inside `renderTeam`. The ReferenceError aborted `renderTeam` partway, so the region list, recent history and everything below stopped drawing: two players reported losing all their regions and an item, and nothing had been lost. Fixed by using `esc2`, and sections are now wrapped so one failure cannot blank the rest, saying plainly that the save is intact.

**Level cap (v85).** `CAPPED_BATTLES` covers gyms, the League, title defences, master rematches, tournaments, Champion's Road, the rival, the evil team from grunt to boss, the legend gauntlet and other players' ghost teams, in any region. Wild creatures, route trainers and legends themselves are untouched.

**Regional forms (v84).** Packs mark variants as extra forms gated on a `gene` condition (0 ordinary, 1 and up regional); the Pokémon pack has 89 species whose forms differ in typing. `regionVariant()` fixes which form a region grows, deterministic per region, species and world seed, so the same species is Dark in one region and Steel in the next and a collection needs travel or trading. `VARIANT.CHANCE` is 0.12, measured at 11.6% of encounters of a variant-capable species, with none ever appearing outside its region. The pack gives forms no stats of their own, so `variantStats()` redistributes the species' own: two stats up, two down, deterministic per species and form, total unchanged within 2%. The displayed name and artwork follow the form already.

**Shiny odds (v82, corrected v83).** Four rates: ordinary scans and eggs 1 in 512 (measured 1 in 461, hunt-family routes excluded), the week's hunt family 1 in 64 (measured 1 in 69), a legend 1 in 128 (measured 1 in 102) and the week's watched legend 1 in 64 (measured 1 in 56). Legends are better than ordinary catches because a player only meets one a day. Each region also watches one of its legends per week, chosen deterministically, and on the day that legend appears its odds are 1 in 64 too (measured 1 in 53 against 1 in 110 for the others). Exactly one legend is watched per region-week, the choice changes week to week, and the roll is still settled when the legend appears so it cannot be rerolled. The approach button turns gold and says "watched this week", and the region's This week tab names it.

**Region borders (v81).** A region's edge was a white hairline, invisible on pale tiles (player report). It is now a 9 px dark casing under a 3.5 px gold dashed line.

**Pin travel (v81).** A pin records `walked: true` only when placed with a real GPS fix, and only such pins can be travelled to. Travel is refused while live GPS is placing the player, so the real position always wins. Arriving sets the position and clears the fix chain, granting no distance, coins or egg progress; scanning, battling and hunting work normally. This answers a player who wanted to walk, mark a hunting spot and return to it from home in the evening without the walking economy being hollowed out.

**Map.** The route fit measures the overlays (trainer chips above, neighbour strip and buttons below) and fits the whole square between them, re-fitting when chips appear; while a guide destination is set, the map instead frames you, the path and the destination together. Pinch-zoom is free; the map fits to the route only until you zoom, then follows at your zoom; Follow me restores.

**Evolution and starting point (v71).** `EVOLVE.ask` (Menu, Playing) makes a level-up record `pendingEvo` and emit `evolveReady` instead of evolving; the battle's end card then offers Evolve or Not yet, and the Team panel keeps the offer until taken. Evolving by hand keeps the creature's share of health and never asks twice for the same step. Any pin can be marked **Start here**: with no live GPS the game opens at that pin rather than where it last left off, which lets a player use it at a desk, or on a device they would rather not give a location to.

**From Discord feedback (v70).** Catch items read Great Ball and Ultra Ball while tier 0 keeps the pack's own word; a Revive costs 8 against a basic ball's 6. Straying redraws the route in about six seconds (35 m off and 60 m moved, or 120 m off outright) and says so aloud. Address search asks for address details and puts door-number matches first, showing the full match before you set off, because a street centroid can sit a kilometre from the house. The six playthroughs are chosen from a list. A creature ready to evolve gets an Evolve button in the Team panel. The speaking voice, its pitch and its speed are chosen from whatever the phone offers. A region can be forgotten from its details page (records go, creatures stay), and the region list sorts by last visit, name, distance, badges or routes with letter and direction filters.

**Turn tracking.** The next instruction is the first step *ahead* of the player's projected position along the route polyline, and its distance is measured along the road. Before v69 it was whichever maneuver was nearest in a straight line, so after taking a turn the app kept counting to the turn just made and the distance grew until the following one came closer (found by Casim driving Kitchener to Wellesley on Highway 7/8). Progress resists snapping backwards (a 60 m leash, 400 m penalty) so a road doubling back cannot put the next turn behind you; remaining distance is along the road while within 80 m of it and straight-line otherwise, and the time left scales with the distance left. Requests are governed, because asking on every GPS fix (every 40 m, so every few seconds in a car) earned an HTTP 429 from the free routers and then a block: one request at a time, at most one per 20 s, a new route only when the player is more than 60 m off the drawn line and has moved 150 m, cache keys rounded to about 100 m so jitter reuses a route, and a doubling pause from 30 s to 5 minutes after each refusal, cleared by a success, a mode change or Retry. Measured: 40 fixes along a route cost one request, leaving the route costs one more, a refusing server gets none, and Retry gets exactly one. Every text box is 16px, because iOS zooms the whole page when a smaller one is focused and never zooms back (this hit Casim while typing an address). Routing tries each server for a mode in turn with a twelve-second timeout, driving falling back to the car-only OSRM demo, and only then draws a straight line, which states the reason and offers a Retry. Transit is a hand-off: buses, trains and ferries need timetables no free worldwide service publishes, so the button opens the phone's maps app in transit mode rather than pretending. Routed by profile: walking, cycling and driving each use their own FOSSGIS router (the ones openstreetmap.org uses), because the OSRM demo server carries only the car network and was returning motorway routes at car speeds for walks; any duration implying more than a profile's ceiling is recomputed from distance. Transit has no public router, so it estimates and offers a hand-off to the phone's Maps app. A compact panel under the header shows the next turn, then distance, duration and arrival clock (75 px; ⋯ expands it), and the map holds the usual route zoom whatever the distance (measured 15.5 with and without the panel) with a gold edge arrow and distance pointing at a destination beyond the screen. Spoken turns are timed rather than fixed: about a minute out, fifteen seconds out, and at the turn, from the smoothed speed of real GPS fixes with a floor per mode. Measured: walking hears 300 m, 90 m and the turn; cycling 400 m; driving at 50 km/h 700 m and 200 m; at 90 km/h a kilometre and a half, then 300 m. Spoken route notes, off by default, announce each new route as it is reached ("Entering Delneor. Route 28. Binacle family here. Dragon gym here, Drake."), which suits a pocket or a cradle. Spoken directions, off by default, read the next turn at 400 m, at 100 m, at the turn and on arrival, skipping the opening "depart" step and never repeating a band; the toggle sits in the panel and the menu. Also shows the modes, with the four modes, Open in Maps, and Whole route (which frames you, the path and the destination; Follow me returns). Follow me centres you and widens symmetrically until the route square fits, so you sit in the middle of the visible gap rather than beside it. One control on the map: a round ◈ button, which becomes a gold pill naming the destination while one is set and clears it when tapped again. It opens with a search box: an address, a place name, or `lat, lon`, geocoded by OpenStreetMap's Nominatim biased to your surroundings, with the nearest matches listed by distance; a pick becomes the destination. Below it, the known targets: the nearest unbeaten gym, the League, today's legend, the contested landmark and the nearest quiet one, your pins, and friends' regions. It asks OSRM's public walking router for a street route (drawn solid, with distance and minutes) and shows the next turn in the header ("Turn right onto Queen Street in 282 m"); the route refreshes when you stray 40 m or after 90 s. If the router is unreachable, the straight line and crow-flies distance stand in. Nothing about the request identifies you beyond the two coordinates it needs. Below zoom 12.5 every region in view is drawn, named, with your badge count where visited.

**Memory.** Per-region record: visits, routes explored, all 8 gyms by name/type/badge, wins with the leader's level and team, champion runs, title holder. History log (cap 400). Region lore: five sentences from typed templates with cross-slot coherence rules and a slop check in the harness. Visited regions drawn on the map below zoom 12.5.

**Stats (light mode).** Power sets the total; for Pokémon packs the six stats are shaped by the species' base-stat profile (embedded table, national dex 1–1025, ratios clamped to 0.35–2.5×), so Shuckle is a wall that cannot hit; attackers use whichever of Atk/Def or SpA/SpD is the better matchup. Packs without a profile keep flat stats.

**Shinies.** 1 in 1024 on scans, eggs and legends; the pack's shiny sprites and icons are used (forms too); the dex counts them.

**Shops (v72).** Malls, department stores, supermarkets, convenience stores, DIY stores and markets are shops. Once visited, standing there opens a small sheet with two to four of the pack's evolution items, stones, Mega Stones or TMs, priced around the pack base, deterministic per place per day: every shop stocks something different and all of them change overnight, which gives a walk across town a point. Marked gold on the map.

**Rest stops.** Landmarks that treat or shelter people (hospitals, clinics, doctors, pharmacies, community centres, places of worship, town halls, libraries, universities) and a steady one in four of the others become rest stops. A player must visit one first; from then on standing there heals the team to full for nothing, as often as they like, and the marker gains a green ring. Finding one heals on the spot and is logged. This answers the feedback that 30-coin heals were too dear for players who cannot walk far.

**Thin regions.** Landmarks are searched in two tiers. The ordinary search asks for the places worth a detour; when a region returns fewer than its target (0.75 per route, floor 30: so 30 for a 36-route Domains region and 61 for an 81-route Wide one), a second, wider search adds everyday places (murals and statues, hospitals and clinics, schools, community centres, galleries, gardens, nature reserves, markets, sports centres, stadiums, playgrounds, picnic sites) and takes up to 120 results, so a quiet neighbourhood still has somewhere to walk to. If the wide search still falls short, unmapped spots top the region up to its target, so a rural region is never empty. A cached region below target is refetched rather than kept for the week. Measured on a mocked region: three landmarks became thirteen.

**Landmarks.** Real places from OpenStreetMap (historic, museums, viewpoints, artworks, places of worship, town halls, libraries, theatres, universities, parks, peaks, lighthouses, towers), fetched per region from the Overpass API and cached for a week; offline, clearly labelled "unmapped spots" keep the system alive. Standing within 60 m: quiet landmarks pay 15 coins and a 20% item once a day; contested ones hold a grunt.

**Evil teams.** Motive-first generator: 36 hand-written motives (weather, relics, debt, purity, signal, wild, crown, night, forge, mask, greed, silence, speed, memory, fame, harvest, ascent, clock, wager, border, catalyst, meteor, ash, arena, famine, poverty, caste, faith, election, excess, contagion, labour, espionage, automaton, tradition, chaos), each with a goal, creed, method, boss bio, motive-bound boss titles, matching core types, and grunt/admin/boss lines; 12 single-word names per motive in the Rocket/Plasma style, no name shared between motives (432 unique teams, then numbered). Supporting types cover the core's weaknesses; admin and boss names come from the type's name pool. Region details show the team's aim, method, creed and boss bio. A per-player book assigns one team per region per week: a team is finished only when its boss falls that week and is then never seen again; an unfinished team returns the next week in its own region or one named neighbour (decided deterministically), and no two regions share a team in a week. They hold max(1, ⌊(n − 2) / 2.5⌋) of the region's n landmarks (cap 6), one being the day's target; free them all and the admin appears at the biggest landmark; beat the admin and, with 8 badges there, the boss (five creatures above the leaders); beat the boss and they leave for the rest of the week. Grunt 30, admin 100, boss 300 coins plus an item.

**Post-game.** Rival named from your starter, appearing at first badge, four, eight and the title, with a team whose ace counters your starter's type, growing 2 → 6. Master rematches once Champion: each beaten leader offers Lv 90 (ace 95) for a gold badge. Region mastery: all routes, all badges, every family on its routes caught. Champion's Road: hold three adjacent titles and defend all three in one weekend for 500 coins. Daily quests: three a day (walk 2 km, beat 3 trainers, catch 2, catch a named type, visit 2 landmarks, win a gym), 40 coins each. Weekly shiny hunt: one family per region at 1 in 64. Weekend tournaments at your League with your live team or a rental of six, three rising rounds, 250 coins. Monthly legend gauntlet on the last weekend: fight all three at the League at champion +20, or chase them across three neighbouring regions, 400 coins.

**Starters.** The pool is the 27 grass/fire/water starters of generations 1–9 (by national dex); three are offered per starting region, one of each type. Other packs fall back to base-stage grass/fire/water creatures.

**Pins.** Personal pins (home, work, exercise, school, other with a name) placed at your current spot, per device, shown on the map and listed in the menu with distance and bearing.

**Held items and battle forms.** Mega Stones Mega Evolve the holder for the duration of a battle (announced on the card, reverted after, HP scaled); other held-item forms (Griseous Orb, Plates on Arceus) are permanent while held. Held items whose pack description says they boost a type give that type's strikes ×1.2, in both battle modes. Creatures track battles fought and last outcome, so Aegislash and Minior change forms by result.

**Music.** `forge-engine.js` (the Musical Forge Studio engine, MIT, shipped beside the app) plays recipes in its own format: root, mode, bpm, prog, bass, drums, lead, dens, bars, seed. One recipe per gym type; a domain differs from another of the same type only by seed; battle variants raise bpm and density, the League is harmonic minor strings at 132. Audio starts on the first tap (browser rule); no volume control in the engine, so the toggle is on/off. A pack may carry its own music (`sounds` entries `area_<type>.mp3`, `area.mp3`, `battle.mp3`, `league.mp3`, also ogg/wav/m4a), or a folder of such files can be attached for a session from the pack loader; pack audio plays ahead of composed themes. Music from other apps is never extracted or shipped. Sound effects (hits, crits, guard, miss, wind-up, faint, catch, fled, level-up, coin, badge) are synthesized inline in raw Web Audio; a wind-up also vibrates where the phone allows.

**Multiplayer (casim-games Supabase project, `wp_` tables).** Auth-free per the backend canon: `wp_register` issues a player token and an 8-character friend code; every other call is a `SECURITY DEFINER` RPC keyed by that token; tables carry RLS with zero policies and revoked grants (anon cannot select them directly, verified by impersonation). Three tiers: (1) ghosts, one team snapshot per player per pack with region, level, badges and title. Ghosts reach you three ways so a small player base still meets people: nearby (within three regions), friends (anywhere, via `wp_friend_ghosts`), and travelling ghosts (the most recent anywhere, via `wp_recent_ghosts`, fetched when fewer than four others are known). Friends and travellers walk your region as trainers, one route per day each, drawn as translucent hovering figures (their lead creature with their name, ✈ for travellers) on both the map and the grid, dimmed once beaten that day; any ghost may come for your title at the League. (2) friends by code and leaderboards per pack (badges, regions, mastery, tournaments, shinies); stats publish after gym, League, defence and tournament wins. (3) live battles: a room code and a shared seed; both phones run the same deterministic `pvpRound` and exchange only per-turn choices (moves, guard, switches) through `wp_submit_choice`, polling every 1.5 s; the harness proves two simulated phones produce identical logs and HP. Shared region weeks (`wp_region_weeks`): when online, the first player to enter a region in a week sets its evil team's seed for everyone, freed landmarks merge across players, and the first admin and boss wins are credited by name in the region's details. Friends' regions show on the zoomed-out map (blue outline, or a blue marker with their name when out of view). "Delete my online data" (`wp_delete_me`) removes the player, ghost, stats and friendships. Client is plain fetch, no library. The Online sheet is four tabs (You: card, scan, ghost, rename, delete, achievements, cloud save; Friends: add by code, friends' stats, leaderboards, who is in your region today; Battle: live rooms; Trade: trades and egg gifts), with the last tab remembered. Privacy audit (2026-09-05): no `wp_` column stores latitude, longitude or accuracy; ghosts carry a region index and name only; pins and photos never leave the phone; only the publishable key ships in the client; every table has RLS with zero policies and no grants to anon; all 27 callable functions are SECURITY DEFINER with a pinned `search_path` and all but `wp_register` and `wp_leaderboard` require the caller's token. Fixed in the same pass: the leaderboard no longer returns friend codes, since a code is an invitation that reveals the holder's region. Handle, token and code live in localStorage (export the save to keep them).

**Creatures.** 16 MBTI-keyed personalities with mood lines. XP bar and stats on every team row and in the battle card. Nicknames (used in battle) and a pin that blocks boxing.

**How to play.** Menu → How to play: fifteen topics (Saving, phones and hosting gathers the save file, the cloud, second-phone linking and the server choice, which were scattered across Playing with others and Settings) (Worlds and playthroughs was split out of Settings when that topic reached four screenfuls). A harness check greps the guide text for 37 features a player must be able to find, so a feature added without documentation fails the tests. Previously thirteen topics behind a picker (getting started, reading the map, directions, scanning and catching, battles and moves, gyms and the League, walking and the shop, legends, landmarks and evil teams, after the title, playing with others, photos and achievements, settings and packs), with Previous/Next and the last topic remembered. Landmark markers answer a tap with name and status.

**Cloud save.** `wp_cloud_save`/`wp_cloud_load`: the whole save under the trainer token, per pack; auto-saved after gym and League wins (at most every 10 minutes) and on demand; load replaces the local save after a confirmation.

**Trades by code.** `wp_trade_offer` gives a six-letter code and removes the creature from your box; a taker gives one of theirs and receives yours; you collect theirs from the Online sheet; cancel returns yours. The wire carries species, level, XP, nickname, TM, held item, gene, gender, shiny, walked distance, battles, personality, where it was caught and the original trainer; the receiver sees "from Casim".

**Weekly report.** The Team panel sums the last seven days from history (km, catches, badges, titles, defences, trainers, landmarks, bosses, tournaments, eggs), the regions visited and this week's team, with a Share button (Web Share or clipboard).

**Map readability.** Your lead creature walks beside your dot facing the direction of travel; a gold ripple marks a catch; wind-ups vibrate.

**Camera and community.** Group photos: any friend's ghost team can be brought into your photo, tagged with their name, and the caption names everyone. Sightings: after beating a friend's or traveller's ghost on your route, a photo with their creature large in frame, captioned with route and date. Trainer cards: a generated 1080×1350 image with name, team, counts, achievement art and a QR code of a card link (`?add=CODE&name=…`), produced by an in-page QR encoder (byte mode, EC L, versions 1–6, verified by an independent decoder); scanning uses the phone's built-in detector where it exists (Android), otherwise the code is typed, and opening a card link pre-fills the friend code. Weekly photo challenge per region: stand at a named landmark or have a member of the hunt family on your team when you take a photo, for 60 coins; three completions earn the Photographer achievement. **Achievements.** Twenty, computed from the save (first badge, full set, champion, defender, reclaimer, gold badge, region master, conference, legend catcher, gauntlet, team breaker, five down, bracket, shine, glitter, collector, walker, hatcher, rivalry, traveller), each drawn with badge-pool art of a fitting type; shown on the Online profile, counted in stats and as a leaderboard.

**Egg gifts.** Send an incubating egg to a friend by code (`wp_gift_offer`/`wp_gift_take`); the receiver walks the remaining distance; nothing comes back.

## Packs

- **GMS .bin** (Tankenka's format): JSON + base64 images + trailing signature the engine ignores. Loads directly; 1,025 Pokémon, 482 families, trainer classes, leader pools, badges, egg data, items.
- **scouter-pack/1** (this project's JSON + sprite folder): supports `light` or `full` battles; `pokeapi-to-pack.mjs` builds a full-mode pack from the PokéAPI CSV dump; `pack-build.mjs` builds from CSVs.
- Saves are per pack **and grid**: the three layouts × two route sizes are six independent playthroughs of the same ground, each with its own team, box, badges, dex, items and history. Coins are the one shared thing (a purse per pack). Switching layout or route size swaps slots, offering a starter in a fresh one and reporting what waits in a used one; the menu lists all six with their team size, level and badges. An older single-slot save migrates once into Domains 500 m. Reset wipes only the current slot and keeps the purse. Cloud saves, exports and imports carry the grid. region, badge, legend and history records are keyed by layout + route size as well, so switching settings hides (never mislabels) records made under other settings, and switching back restores them.

## Distribution plan (decided 2026-09-04, not legal advice)

A store build cannot contain franchise packs (Apple IP/copycat rules, Google Play flagging, Nintendo/TPC takedowns; pointing at a URL you host for that purpose reads the same to reviewers). Plan:

1. **Store build = WayPack + an original starter pack + loaders.** The app must be a complete game without external content. The original pack comes from Lumoria's creatures when Lumoria is ready (region-forge is Pokémon-bound and cannot supply it); badge art from the region-forge pool is Casim's own.
2. **Franchise packs stay outside the store**, hosted on Discord/GitHub by whoever makes them (Tankenka's model). Engine keeps file loading and gains **load from a user-typed URL**; a pack-format guide for community authors is an engineering job.
3. **GitHub Pages build** stays the playground: no review, anything Casim is willing to host; no background GPS (that needs the Capacitor wrap, which is a store build).
